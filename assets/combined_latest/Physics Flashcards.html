Physics Flashcards

Card 1

Q: What is the formula for the magnitude of the electrostatic force between two point charges according to Coulomb's Law?

A: $F = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}$

Card 2

Q: In the context of the electric field, what is the value of the constant $\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$ in SI units?

A: $9 \times 10^9 \text{ N} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$

Card 3

Q: How is electric field intensity $\vec{E}$ defined in terms of the force $\vec{F}$ on a test charge $q_0$?

A: $\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q_0}$

Card 4

Q: State the integral form of Gauss's Law for an electric field in a vacuum.

A: $\Phi_e = \oint_S \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{S} = \frac{1}{\epsilon_0} \sum_{i=1}^n q_i^{in}$

Card 5

Q: True or False: The electric field $\vec{E}$ in the Gauss's Law integral represents only the field generated by charges inside the Gaussian surface.

A: False; it represents the total field from all charges both inside and outside the surface.

Card 6

Q: Which charges contribute to the net electric flux $\Phi_e$ through a closed Gaussian surface?

A: Only the charges enclosed within the Gaussian surface ($q_i^{in}$).

Card 7

Q: What is the conventional sign for electric flux that is 'leaving' or 'piercing out' of a closed surface?

A: Positive

Card 8

Q: When calculating the electric field of a continuous charge distribution, what is the expression for $dq$ in terms of volume charge density $\rho$?

A: $dq = \rho dV$

Card 9

Q: What is the relationship between the electric field $E$ just outside the surface of a conductor in equilibrium and the surface charge density $\sigma$?

A: $E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$

Card 10

Q: What is the magnitude of the electric field $\vec{E}$ inside a solid conductor in electrostatic equilibrium?

A: Zero

Card 11

Q: Where does the excess charge reside in a solid conductor that has reached electrostatic equilibrium?

A: Entirely on the outer surface of the conductor.

Card 12

Q: In electrostatic equilibrium, what is the orientation of the electric field $\vec{E}$ relative to the conductor's surface at any point?

A: It is always perpendicular (normal) to the surface.

Card 13

Q: How does the surface charge density $\sigma$ on an irregularly shaped conductor relate to the local radius of curvature?

A: The density $\sigma$ is higher where the radius of curvature is smaller (sharper points).

Card 14

Q: What is the effect of an external electric field on a dielectric material?

A: It causes polarization, creating polarization charges on the surface which weaken the internal field.

Card 15

Q: How is the electric field $E$ inside a dielectric related to the vacuum field $E_0$ and the relative permittivity $\epsilon_r$?

A: $E = \frac{E_0}{\epsilon_r}$

Card 16

Q: What physical quantity does the electric dipole moment $\vec{p}$ represent for a pair of opposite charges $\pm q$ separated by distance $\vec{r}_0$?

A: $\vec{p} = q\vec{r}_0$

Card 17

Q: State the two fundamental postulates of Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity.

A: The principle of relativity and the principle of the constancy of the speed of light.

Card 18

Q: In the Lorentz transformation, what is the formula for the Lorentz factor $\gamma$?

A: $\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}$, where $\beta = v/c$.

Card 19

Q: Write the Lorentz coordinate transformation for $x'$ in terms of the stationary frame coordinates $(x, t)$.

A: $x' = \gamma(x - vt)$

Card 20

Q: Write the Lorentz coordinate transformation for $t'$ in terms of the stationary frame coordinates $(x, t)$.

A: $t' = \gamma(t - \frac{v}{c^2}x)$

Card 21

Q: What is the 'Relativity of Simultaneity' conclusion regarding two events occurring at different locations?

A: Events simultaneous in one inertial frame are generally not simultaneous in another frame moving relative to the first.

Card 22

Q: Under what specific condition will two events that are simultaneous in one frame also be simultaneous in all other inertial frames?

A: When the two events occur at the same location (same spatial coordinates).

Card 23

Q: What is the formula for Length Contraction, where $l_0$ is the proper length?

A: $l = l_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$

Card 24

Q: In which direction does length contraction occur relative to the direction of motion?

A: Only in the direction parallel to the motion.

Card 25

Q: What is the formula for Time Dilation, where $\Delta t'$ is the proper time interval?

A: $\Delta t = \frac{\Delta t'}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}}$

Card 26

Q: According to Special Relativity, how does the mass $m$ of an object change as its velocity $v$ increases?

A: $m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}}$

Card 27

Q: What is the formula for relativistic momentum $\vec{p}$?

A: $\vec{p} = m\vec{v} = \frac{m_0\vec{v}}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}}$

Card 28

Q: State Einstein's famous mass-energy equivalence formula for total energy $E$.

A: $E = mc^2$

Card 29

Q: How is relativistic kinetic energy $E_k$ calculated in terms of total energy and rest energy?

A: $E_k = mc^2 - m_0c^2$

Card 30

Q: What is the invariant relation between total energy $E$, momentum $p$, and rest energy $E_0$?

A: $E^2 = E_0^2 + p^2c^2$

Card 31

Q: What is the rest mass $m_0$ of a photon?

A: Zero

Card 32

Q: How is the momentum $p$ of a photon related to its wavelength $\lambda$?

A: $p = \frac{h}{\lambda}$

Card 33

Q: What does the integral of the square of the absolute value of the wavefunction $\int |\psi|^2 dV = 1$ represent?

A: The normalization condition, stating the total probability of finding the particle in all space is 1.

Card 34

Q: State Gauss's Law for Magnetism in integral form.

A: $\oint_S \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{S} = 0$

Card 35

Q: What is the physical implication of the fact that the net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero?

A: Magnetic monopoles do not exist; magnetic field lines always form closed loops.

Card 36

Q: What is the generalized form of Ampere's Law (including displacement current $i_d$)?

A: $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 (i_c + i_d)$

Card 37

Q: In an $L-C$ circuit, what is the second-order differential equation for charge $q$ when resistance $R=0$?

A: $\frac{d^2q}{dt^2} + \frac{1}{LC}q = 0$

Card 38

Q: What is the expression for the energy $E$ of a photon in terms of its frequency $\nu$?

A: $E = h\nu$

Card 39

Q: When applying Gauss's Law, what must be true about the chosen Gaussian surface?

A: It must be a closed surface.

Card 40

Q: What happens to the Lorentz transformation equations when the relative velocity $v \ll c$?

A: They reduce to the classical Galilean transformation equations.

Card 41

Q: Concept: Proper Length ($l_0$)

A: Definition: The length of an object measured in the rest frame where the object is stationary.

Card 42

Q: Concept: Proper Time ($\Delta t'$)

A: Definition: The time interval between two events measured by a clock at rest relative to those events.

Card 43

Q: Why is it impossible for a particle with a non-zero rest mass to reach the speed of light $c$?

A: Because its relativistic mass and energy would become infinite as $v \rightarrow c$.

Card 44

Q: In the context of nuclear physics, what does $\Delta E = \Delta m \cdot c^2$ represent?

A: The energy released (or absorbed) due to a mass defect $\Delta m$ during a nuclear reaction.

Card 45

Q: Roughly how much energy is released in the fission of one $^{235}\text{U}$ nucleus?

A: Approximately $200 \text{ MeV}$

Card 46

Q: What is the expression for the differential electric field $d\vec{E}$ generated by a point charge element $dq$?

A: $d\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{dq}{r^2} \vec{e}_r$

Card 47

Q: In the study of conductors, what is 'Electrostatic Shielding'?

A: The phenomenon where the interior of a hollow conductor remains free from the influence of external electric fields.

Card 48

Q: What determines the direction of the force on a charge in an electric field?

A: The sign of the charge; positive charges feel force in the direction of $\vec{E}$, negative charges opposite.

Card 49

Q: How does the relativistic momentum formula change when the speed $v$ is very small compared to $c$?

A: It approaches the classical definition $\vec{p} = m_0\vec{v}$.

Card 50

Q: What is the 'rest energy' of a particle with rest mass $m_0$?

A: $E_0 = m_0c^2$

Card 51

Q: How is the surface charge density $\sigma$ defined for a charge $q$ distributed over an area $S$?

A: $\sigma = \frac{dq}{dS}$

Card 52

Q: True or False: A Gaussian surface must correspond to the physical surface of a real object.

A: False; it is an imaginary surface used for mathematical calculation.

Card 53

Q: What is the unit of electric field intensity?

A: $\text{N/C}$ (Newtons per Coulomb) or $\text{V/m}$ (Volts per meter).

Card 54

Q: In the formula $dq = \lambda dl$, what does $\lambda$ represent?

A: Linear charge density.

Card 55

Q: Under what condition can the integral $\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{S}$ be simplified to $EA$?

A: When $E$ is constant in magnitude and perpendicular to the surface at every point.

Card 56

Q: What is the 'Principle of Superposition' for electric fields?

A: The total electric field at a point is the vector sum of the fields produced by each individual charge.

Card 57

Q: What characterizes a 'stationary state' in quantum mechanics according to the provided text?

A: A state with a definite energy $E$ where the probability density is independent of time.

Card 58

Q: What is the relationship between the permitivity of free space $\epsilon_0$ and the Coulomb constant $k$?

A: $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$

Card 59

Q: In a nuclear fusion reaction of hydrogen into helium, is energy absorbed or released?

A: Released, due to the mass of the resulting helium nucleus being less than the sum of the hydrogen nuclei.

Card 60

Q: Relativity Theory implies that space and time are not independent but form an inseparable 4D manifold called _____.

A: Spacetime

Card 61

Q: What does the term 'Isotropic' imply about the speed of light in vacuum?

A: It is the same in all directions regardless of the motion of the source or observer.

Card 62

Q: Common Error: Using Gauss's Law to find $\vec{E}$ for a system with low symmetry. Why is this difficult?

A: Because the electric field magnitude $E$ cannot be easily pulled out of the dot product integral.

Card 63

Q: Common Error: Forgetting to account for the direction of the vector $d\vec{S}$ in flux calculations. Which way does it point?

A: It always points outward from the interior of a closed volume.

Card 64

Q: Concept Check: If a charge is placed outside a closed Gaussian surface, what is its contribution to the net flux?

A: Zero, because every field line entering the surface must also leave it.

Card 65

Q: Identify the error: 'A moving rod appears longer to a stationary observer.' Correct this statement.

A: A moving rod appears shorter to a stationary observer due to length contraction.

Card 66

Q: In the Lorentz transformation, what happens to the $y$ and $z$ coordinates if motion is along the $x$-axis?

A: They remain unchanged ($y' = y, z' = z$).

Card 67

Q: Formula: What is the relationship between $\beta$ and $\gamma$ in relativity?

A: $\gamma = (1 - \beta^2)^{-1/2}$

Card 68

Q: What is the SI unit for electric charge?

A: Coulomb (C)

Card 69

Q: Applicability: When is the formula $E = mc^2$ valid?

A: It is a general law valid for all forms of matter and energy in all frames of reference.

Card 70

Q: Distinction: How does 'Rest Mass' differ from 'Relativistic Mass'?

A: Rest mass is an intrinsic constant measured at $v=0$; relativistic mass increases with velocity.

Card 71

Q: Process: To find the electric field of a continuous line charge, one should first define a small element _____ and then integrate.

A: $dq = \lambda dl$

Card 72

Q: What is the charge of a single electron (elementary charge $e$)?

A: $1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
